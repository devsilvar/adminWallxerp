Under the Nigeria Tax Act (NTA), the tax rules for businesses depend heavily on your legal structure (Sole Proprietorship vs. LLC) and your financial scale. [1, 2, 3, 4]

1. Structural Breakdown: Business Name vs. LLC
Sole Proprietorships & Registered Business Names
If you operate as an individual, shop owner, or registered Business Name, you do not pay Company Income Tax (CIT). Instead, your business income is treated as personal income and taxed via Personal Income Tax (PIT). [1, 2]
Tax Free Threshold: The first ₦800,000 of your annual profit is completely tax-free.
Tax Rates: Any profit above ₦800,000 is taxed on a progressive scale starting from 7% up to 25% for high earners.
Key Change: The old "Consolidated Relief Allowance" has been removed and replaced with a targeted Rent Relief (deduction of 20% of your rent or ₦500,000, whichever is lower). [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]
Limited Liability Companies (LLC)
LLCs are separate legal entities and are taxed under Company Income Tax (CIT). [1, 2]
Small LLCs (Turnover ≤ ₦100M AND Fixed Assets ≤ ₦250M): You pay 0% CIT. You are also exempt from Capital Gains Tax and the new 4% Development Levy. [1, 2]
Exclusion Warning: If your LLC provides professional services (Consulting, Law, Accounting, Medical), you cannot claim the 0% rate, regardless of how small your turnover is. [1]
Large LLCs: If your turnover exceeds ₦100M or your assets exceed ₦250M, you pay 30% CIT plus a 4% Development Levy on profits. [1, 2]

2. Money & Transaction Taxes (What applies to Everyone)
Even if your business qualifies for 0% Income Tax, you are not exempt from transaction-based taxes: [1]
Value Added Tax (VAT): The standard rate remains 7.5%. However, small businesses with a annual turnover of ₦50 million or less are totally exempt from charging or collecting VAT. Basic food, healthcare, and education items are zero-rated (0% VAT) for everyone. [1, 2, 3]
PAYE (Pay As You Earn): If your business has employees earning more than ₦800,000 annually, you must deduct tax from their monthly salaries and remit it to your State Internal Revenue Service (SIRS). [1, 2]

4. Critical Questions Every Business Owner Must Ask
To avoid tax penalties and clear compliance audits, review these internal questions:

"Are we tracking actual profit or just total turnover?" Taxes are calculated on net profit, not total inflows. If you don't keep receipts for "allowable business expenses" (rent, electricity, supplier costs), the tax office will assess you on a high presumptive rate.
"Do we separate personal money from business money?" Co-mingling funds in a personal bank account makes it look like personal income, which immediately triggers personal income tax rates up to 25% on everything

🏢 Crucial Rules for Businesses with Branches
If your business operates across multiple locations or states, the new tax framework enforces centralized reporting with localized distribution:
Single TIN Rule: Your business has exactly one Tax Identification Number (TIN) tied to your headquarters. You do not generate new TINs for branches. [1]
Centralized filing, Split Remittance: For LLCs, your financial reporting goes straight to the federal Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) as one single corporate entity. However, your operational branch location matters immensely for PAYE (Staff Personal Taxes) and Withholding Tax (WHT):
PAYE Allocation: You must remit your employee payroll tax directly to the specific State Internal Revenue Service (SIRS) where that specific branch operates and where the employee resides.
The Consumption/VAT Trap: Even if your main office is in a tax-exempt tier, your branches must adhere to localized e-invoicing. The NRS monitors automated Point of Sale (POS) and corporate banking data across all branch channels to prevent revenue fragmentation. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]



🌾 Exactly Which Businesses are Fully Exempt?
The law grants sweeping exemptions to lower operating costs, but it requires explicit structural definitions: [1]
Qualifying Small Companies & MSMEs:
The Rule: LLCs with annual turnover ≤ ₦100 million AND fixed assets ≤ ₦250 million.
The Exemptions: 0% Companies Income Tax (CIT), 0% Capital Gains Tax (CGT), and a 100% waiver from the new 4% Development Levy. [1, 2, 3]
Agricultural and Agribusiness Startups:
The Rule: Newly incorporated companies engaged in primary farming, livestock production, or agricultural processing.
The Exemption: A structured 5-year tax holiday (income tax exemption) to bolster national food security. [1, 2, 3]
Export-Oriented Businesses:
The Rule: Firms that generate 100% of their revenue from foreign exports.
The Exemption: Profits are fully exempt from income tax, provided all foreign proceeds are legally repatriated back to Nigeria via official banking channels. [1]
Low-Income Earners & Artisans:
The Rule: Individuals, sole proprietors, or employees earning an annual gross income of ₦800,000 or less.
The Exemption: 100% exempt from personal income tax or PAYE. [1, 2]

⚠️ The Dangerous Exclusions (Who Can NOT Claim Exemptions?)
Do not fall into the trap of thinking your small turnover makes you safe. The following businesses are legally barred from small business exemptions and are taxed on every single Naira from day one: [1]
Professional Services: Any entity operating in consulting, law, bookkeeping/accounting, engineering, or medical services. [1]
Regulated or Financial Businesses: Small fintech startups, money lenders, or investment boutiques do not qualify for the 0% CIT bracket. [1]
The Branch Aggregation Rules: If you own five separate small business names or branches that make ₦25 million each, the NRS aggregates them as one entity making ₦125 million, immediately kicking you out of the small business exemption bracket.

🕒 The "Don't Miss Out" Mandatory Action List
To remain fully safe under the current 2026 digital enforcement protocols:
File Zero Returns: If your LLC qualifies for 0% CIT, you must still file an annual return showing ₦0 tax payable. Failing to do so triggers an instant automated digital penalty starting at ₦100,000. [1, 2]
Implement E-Invoicing: All retail businesses and branches must link their transaction software to the NRS portal for real-time VAT tracking. [1, 2]
Claim Your VAT Input Credits: For the first time, if your business pays VAT on raw services, utilities, or capital equipment, you can legally deduct and recover that VAT against the VAT you collect from customers. Do not leave this money on the table. [1, 2]


1. The 1% "Presumptive Tax" for Nano and Informal Businesses
If you operate an unregistered business, a tiny retail shop, or are a freelance hustle operator with low income records, the government has regularized the informal sector via the 2026 Presumptive Tax Regulations: [1]
Turnover under ₦12 Million: Your business is categorized as a Nano/Petite business. You are eligible for a free, annually renewable tax exemption certificate from your State Revenue Service. [1]
Turnover above ₦12 Million (with no proper accounting books): If you fail to keep structured financial statements but your account velocity is high, the state tax authority will apply a flat 1% presumptive tax on your gross turnover. They calculate this based on your lifestyle, business location, and transaction volumes. [1]
2. Automated Bank Account Audits
The Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) is fully integrated with commercial banks, FinTech platforms (like Moniepoint, OPay, PalmPay), and POS provider databases. [1]
The Flagging System: The system monitors your Tax Identification Number (TIN) activity. If your inflows cross the small business limits (₦100 Million turnover), the system automatically strips your tax-exempt status, and you will receive an automated corporate tax bill. [1, 2]
The Solution: Ensure your personal inflows (family gifts, personal loans) are never mixed into your corporate account, as the system cannot differentiate personal deposits from business turnover.
3. Total Ban on Cash Tax Collections and Roadblocks
To stop harassment of small businesses transporting goods across state borders or managing local branches, the law explicitly bans all forms of cash tax collections, unauthorized local revenue agents, and fiscal roadblocks. All legitimate tax payments—federal, state, or local government—must be executed strictly through electronic and digital channels. Any agent demanding physical cash at your shop or on the road is operating illegally. [1]
4. Six-Year Back-Audit Penalty Rule
Under the Nigeria Tax Administration Act, the NRS maintains a 6-year window to review your business records. If you falsely claim to be a "Small Company" or hide branch revenues today, and an audit discovers the truth 3 or 4 years down the line, they will back-date the 30% corporate income tax, add a mandatory 10% penalty, and charge interest at the prevailing Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) Monetary Policy Rate. Option B (Limited Liability Company - LLC): Best if you have partners or plan to raise capital. This shields your personal assets and unlocks the 0% Corporate Income Tax exemption if your turnover stays under ₦100 million.

Verification: Visit the Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) TaxPro Max portal to search for your company name and confirm that your TIN is active.

The Golden Rule: Never mix business revenue with personal funds.
Linking: Provide your CAC documents and your active TIN to your bank or preferred commercial FinTech platform to open a corporate business account. This links your banking trail to your official tax profile.
📝 Step 4: Configure Your Invoicing & Withholding Tax (WHT)
Display Your TIN: Clearly print your Tax Identification Number on all your client invoices and receipts.

Total Monthly Inflows: (To prove you are safely below the ₦50M VAT threshold and the ₦100M CIT threshold).
Allowable Expenses: Save receipts for rent, stock, equipment purchases, utility bills, and internet costs.

Monthly Deadlines: If you employ staff, you must remit PAYE tax to your State Internal Revenue Service by the 10th of every month.
